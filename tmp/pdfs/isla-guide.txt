This capstone project, “Instructional Sipnayan Learning Adventure (I.S.L.A.): A 2D 
Game-based Mobile Learning System with Progress Analytics for Grade 3 Mathematics at 
Master Hand School Inc.,” intends to be a supplemental learning tool that motivates Grade 
3 students and practice their mathematics performance through adventure gameplay  with 
time-limited combat mechanics . The introduction outlines the students’ difficulty with 
math, the manual recording of their progress, and the objectives, scope, and limitations of 
the study, including pre - and post -assessment results. The review of related literature 
supports game -based learning as a way to strengthen numeracy, motivation, and 
engagement. The methodology covers the technical background, requirements, and system 
design, and describes the Agile approach used to build the Android game with Godot 
Engine and the web dashboar d with analytics. In the game, students explore six math 
islands, defeat enemies through time -limited multiple -choice quiz combat, and earn 
badges, while teachers and administrators monitor progress and manage accounts and 
questions through the web platform. The results a nd discussion present the testing, 
prototype, and implementation results. The conclusion summarizes the key findings and 
suggests future improvements.

INTRODUCTION 
Project Context 
The study involves Grade 3 students from Master Hand School Inc., focusing on the 
challenges they encounter in learning fundamental mathematics and the need for a 
supplementary learning tool that supports the Department of Education's (DepEd) 
MATATAG Curri culum. According to the Second Congressional Commission on 
Education (EDCOM II), as reported by GMA News (2024), Filipino elementary learners 
continue to experience difficulties in foundational numeracy, highlighting the need for 
approaches that strengthen  mathematical understanding and problem -solving skills.The 
Researchers conducted an interview with Ms. Angela Babe Gungon, a Grade 3 teacher at 
Master Hand School Inc., regarding the students' mathematics learning experiences and the 
teaching methods used at the institution. The interview covered the common difficulties 
students encounter when solving mathematical problems, their interest and participation 
during classroom discussions, the effectiveness of interactive learning activities, and the 
mathematical skills that should be reinforced through the proposed game. Additionally, the 
Researchers conducted a preliminary survey among Grade 3 students regarding their 
mathematics learning habits, frequency of practicing mathematics outside school hours, 
and interest in educational adventure games.  Based on the interview, Ms. Gungon  
explained that mathematics lessons are primarily delivered through traditional lectures and 
that students tend to lose interest when discussions consist mainly of numbers and text. She 
also noted that students frequently experience difficulties interpreti ng mathematical 
questions and performing calculations correctly. However, students demonstrated greater 
enthusiasm and were able to recall lessons more easily when interactive activities were 
incorporated into classroom discussions. The preliminary survey also indicated that 
students did not consistently practice mathematics at home but expressed interest in 
adventure-based educational games. To address these concerns, Instructional Sipnayan 
Learning Adventure (I.S.L.A.): A 2D Game -based Mobile Learning System with 
Progress Analytics for Grade 3 Mathematics at Master Hand School Inc.  developed 
by the researchers to motivate Grade 3 students and improve their performance in learning 
mathematics through engaging narrative-driven adventure gameplay. The title reflects the

Purpose and Description 
The purpose of “Instructional Sipnayan Learning Adventure (I.S.L.A.): A 2D Game-
based mobile learning system with progress analytics for Grade 3 Mathematics at Master 
Hand School Inc.”, is to support Grade 3 students to actively engage in mathematics as a 
supplementary tool to support existing  teaching methods. The Game is 2D Adventure 
game, focusing on the theme of Mathematics. The Game will provide knowledge about 
Foundational mathematics through exploration, and interactive quizzes as the combat 
mechanic. The Game will allow the student to play as the student of “Professor Cal”, The 
one who will guide them throughout the “Sipnayan archipelago”, the setting of the game.  
I.S.L.A. is a 2D adventure game set in an archipelago of "math islands," each 
representing a specific Grade 3 mathematics topic. Students control a character to explore 
these islands and encounter enemies, which trigger mathematical challenges. Each 
encounter presents a multiple -choice question that the player must answer correctly to 
defeat the enemy, and players must complete multiple quests to progress through the story 
of the game. 
With I.S.L.A., students learn math through an engaging game‑based environment. 
Answering challenges correctly encourages them to pay closer attention in class to advance 
further—creating a bridge between home and school. Through progressive practice and 
repeated challenges, students build skills and confidence, driven by intrinsic motivation 
rather than instruction alone. I.S.L.A. transforms math from a passive subject into an 
adventure they genuinely want to pursue.

General Problem 
The Grade 3 students’ difficulty in solving math problems(e.g., multiplication and 
division), and no dedicated system to assess their math skill and performance. 
Based on conducted surveys and teacher interviews at Master Hand School Inc., 
Grade 3 students exhibit insufficient motivation to engage in mathematics learning, causing 
them to lose interest and rarely practice math at home. Survey data reveals that 63% of  
students report practicing math only "sometimes" outside school hours. Teachers note that 
traditional instruction —or lessons that are "purely numbers" —lacks the interactive 
elements necessary to sustain student engagement, resulting in passive learning an d 
minimal independent practice.  
Specific Problems 
1. Grade 3 Students’ Decreasing Motivation and Struggle with Abstract 
Mathematics. 
Mathematics lessons at Master Hand School Inc. are primarily 
lecture‑based, causing students to lose interest when instruction is purely 
numbers and text. This difficulty  in sustaining engagement extend to home 
habits: a survey of 29 Grade 3 students found that 64% practice math at home 
only sometimes and 3% never, yet most want an adventure game. Nationally, 
EDCOM II reports that only one in four Grade 3 students can perform division 
correctly. Rohim et al. (2024) and Yulia et al. (2025) confirm that insufficient 
motivation and lack of interactive methods are key obstacles, requiring digital 
means to sustain learning interest from classroom to home. Without an 
engaging adventure game, students’ in terest may weaken and their difficulty 
with foundational operations will persist. 
2. Manual Recording of Game-Based Math Progress is Inefficient. 
Teachers currently use manual grade books or Microsoft Excel to record 
student performance at fixed points. These methods do not provide continuous 
visibility into how Grade 3 students progress through game‑based math 
activities. Recording island completions, battle accuracy, and topic mastery by 
hand is inefficient.

3. Paper-Based Math Assessments Do Not Offer Interactive or Adaptive 
Feedback. 
The teacher measures the student’s math skills through paper‑based 
activities. They do not provide ongoing, adaptive feedback or gradually 
increase difficulty as the student progresses. Without an interactive, 
game‑embedded assessment tool that turns mistak es into learning moments, 
young learners miss the chance to build foundational math skills 
incrementally—making assessment feel static rather than a continuous, 
engaging journey for digitally oriented learners. 
 
 
General Objective 
To Develop I.S.L.A., A 2D Game-Based Mobile Learning System with Progress 
Analytics for Grade 3 Mathematics at Master Hand School Inc.  
The system aims to i mprove the Grade 3 students’ mathematics performance and 
engagement through a game‑based approach aligned with the curriculum, while also 
providing teachers with a web‑based analytics dashboard. 
Specific Objectives 
1. To Develop A 2D Adventure Game That Embeds Grade 3 Mathematics 
into Gameplay and Prove Its Effectiveness through Pre -and Post 
Assessment. 
The objective is to create an engaging, game -based supplementary tool 
and evaluate its effectiveness in improving mathematics performance by at 
least 20% through pre- and post-assessments. Students will be able to explore 
an interactive environment and prac tice mathematics in the “Sipnayan  
Archipelago,” which focuses on Grade 3 mathematics, while playing as 
apprentices of Professor Cal. Game objects refer to interactive elements such 
as badges and NPCs, which provide a sense of accomplishment and motivate 
students to continue “playing while learning.” Through the game, students can 
learn and practice solving various Grade 3 mathematics problems.

2. To develop a web dashboard that tracks and displays each student’s in -
game progress across math islands. 
The system display students’ in‑game progress across math islands, 
including mastery levels, battle statistics (wins, losses, accuracy, time taken), 
and engagement indicators based on time spent per activity, using only game 
interaction data without monitoring students directly.  
3. To d evelop a game feature that assesses the student’s mathematics 
knowledge  through a combat system with quiz mechanics. 
The combat system embeds Grade 3 math curriculum into battles against 
enemies called “Algemon” . Correct answers deal damage; incorrect answers 
carry mild in-game consequences but no permanent failure. To avoid cheating, 
questions are randomized per attempt and response timing is monitored. Unlike 
leaderboard platforms (e.g., Kahoot), this mechanic offers a personal adventure 
where each answer affects battle progress. Mistakes become learning moments, 
and defeating an Algemon rewards accomplishment, motivating continued 
practice within a risk-free, goal-driven island setting.

REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE/STUDIES/SYSTEMS 
Local RRL 
According to Digital Game-Based Learning: Its Contribution to Numeracy Skills 
among Grade 1 Learners  by Buray and Alferez (2025), learning through digital games 
was a learner -centered method, where instructional objectives and materials were 
interwoven in gaming activities and inspired learners to study and advance their abilities 
and expertise by giving them fun and engaging experience surroundings for learning. The 
study was conducted at Andales Integrated School, Initao  North District, Division of 
Misamis Oriental during the Second Quarter of the School Year 2024 -2025, utilizing a 
quasi-experimental design with 34 Grade 1 participants. Due to the growing interest of 
children in playing video games, the usage of these gam es has become a hot topic, and 
incorporating digital instructional games into the curriculum improved learners accessed 
to the material and boosts their performance noticeably. Its findings remain highly relevant 
to Grade 3 students who may still exhibit gaps in foundational numeracy skills, as gaining 
a strong conceptual grasp of fundamental numeracy ideas like counting, recognizing 
numbers, and performing simple addition and subtraction arithmetic operations can be 
difficult, and understanding the signifi cance and connections between numbers may be 
difficult for certain learners. The results demonstrated that Digital Game -Based Learning 
(DGBL) effectively enhanced numeracy skills, as evidenced by significant post -test 
improvements and a shift toward higher proficiency levels.  
 
According to Using Mobile Technology to Promote Higher-Order Thinking Skills 
in Elementary Mathematics by Verzosa et al. (2021), the researchers have examined that 
the usage of mobile applications as instructional tools, arguing that "mobile technology can 
provide a potential solution, especially when application (app) design is based on sound 
pedagogical principles and gamification elements" and that it could serve as a means  to 
enhance elementary students' mathematical skills, particularly in their foundational 
arithmetic operations such as addition and subtraction. The study aimed to integrate mobile 
technology into how mathematics, focusing on the foundational mathematical concepts 
that directly aligns with the developmental stage of the learners in the Philippines. By

According to Leveling Up Mathematical Skills: The Effectiveness of Game-Based 
Learning by Cayang and Ursabia (2024), the study utilized an experimental approach to 
determine the effectiveness of game -based learning (GBL) in teaching mathematics to 
Grade 4 students in Aleosan , North Cotabato, Philippines. The research revealed that the 
experimental group's mean mathematics score increased from 11.33 in the pretest to 19.18 
in the posttest, representing an approximately 69.28% relative improvement. Furthermore, 
the experimental  group obtained a higher posttest mean score (19.18) than the control 
group (16.31), with a statistically significant difference (p = 0.022). These findings 
demonstrate that game -based learning can contribute to measurable improvements in 
mathematics performance compared with conventional teaching methods. 
 
The study emphasizes that GBL is a valuable strategy for enhancing mathematics 
proficiency by transforming traditional exercises into interactive learning experiences. It 
highlights the importance of student -centered activities that encourage participation,  
problem-solving, and learning from mistakes. By integrating challenges and opportunities

Synthesis 
There is a great need for game-based mathematics intervention among Filipino Grade 
3 students. National data reveal that only one in four can correctly perform division by the 
end of the school year (EDCOM II, 2024), while classroom -level studies report mean 
percentage scores of 38.20% in foundational operations and a critically low 20.60% in 
problem-solving—both classified as non-numerate (Celemin, 2023). Traditional, teacher -
centered instruction has been shown to foster disinterest and mathematics anxiety, 
compounding these performance deficits (Balaoing & Say, 2024).  Game-based learning 
offers a validated alternative. Research consistently demonstrates that digital games 
increase motivation, conceptual retention, and higher -order thinking by embedding 
progressive difficulty, immediate feedback, and a “play while learning” atmosphere (Yulia 
et al., 2025; Dan et al., 2024). Even brief sessions of 15 to 20 minutes produce measurable 
gains (DePascale & Ramani, 2025). Notable examples highlight the versatility of this 
approach: narrative-driven 2D platformers like MiniX  and Math Adventures with Tuffy 
sustain immersion and arithmetic skill retention through quests and combat (Kulkarni et 
al., 2024; Priyanka, 2025) . Despite these advances, no  existing Grade 3 tool integrates a 
narrative 2D adventure, mobile (Android) accessibility, time-limited combat quiz 
mechanics, progressive difficulty across all math topics, risk -free failure, and a teacher 
progress analytics dashboard within a single platform. I.S.L.A. directly addresses this gap 
by transforming mathematics practice into a personal, motivating journey: learners solve  
problems to battle  enemies, where correct answers deal damage, mistakes become 
immediate learning moments, and defeating enemies brings meaningful in -game 
accomplishment.

Technologies to be Used 
To develop the I.S.L.A. game, a combination of tools and technologies is 
utilized to handle different aspects of the system. The core of the game is built using 
Godot Engine, which manages the 2D environment, scene structure, navigation,

enemy state machines, math battle mechanics, and save management are 
programmed using GDScript, allowing efficient and structured implementation 
within the engine. Visual assets such as character and enemy sprites, designs,

wireframing. For backend data management, Firebase handles user account 
authentication, and handles storage, and synchronization of player saves securely 
in the cloud. Math questions and answers for each topic are organized and stored in 
JSON files, which are loaded and randomized during gameplay battles to ensure 
variety and unpredictability. In addition, Canva supports the creation of visual 
materials for presentations, promotional content, and interface concept designs, 
while draw.io is used to produce system diagrams that illustrate the structure and 
processes of the system. Visual Studio Code  will be used for web development 
part of the system. Finally, GitHub serves as the primary version control platform, 
enabling the researchers to manage code changes, track revision history, and 
maintain a stable and organized codebase throughout all phases of development  
with Vercel serving as the deployment platform for the connected Git repository to 
host the project's web dashboard.

planned as the first external service to integrate.

Requirements Analysis 
The I.S.L.A. game is designed for Grade 3 students as the primary users. Students 
interact directly with the mobile application, while the teacher utilize the web application 
to monitor student performance and progress. 
The game focuses on improving students’ understanding of fundamental Grade 3 
mathematics.  
The game integrates a 2D adventure game with time-limited combat mechanics approach 
in which students explore an island environment, encounter enemies, and solve multiple -
choice math problems through a quiz -based battle interface to progress. This progression 
allows students to unlock chapters or islands, each consisting of thre e quests: easy, 
medium, and hard.  
The game is played as supplementary learning tool. It is designed with simplicity in mind, 
enabling consistent practice without overwhelming students, even for those with limited 
gaming experience. 
The game intentionally omits leaderboards and grading to prevent competitiveness that 
may lead to external assistance from unintended users, and to discourage speedrunning 
behaviors or cheating that could undermine the game's learning objectives. 
The system includes a website that provides analytics for teachers, as well as user 
management and question-and-choice management for administrators. It also serves as the 
platform where students can download the I.S.L.A. game.  
The website is primarily used by teachers to monitor students’ progress in the game, 
including metrics such as scores, time taken, accuracy, difficulty level, and topic type. This 
allows them to identify students’ strengths and areas that require improvement. It also helps 
teachers make informed decisions in adjusting instruction and providing targeted support 
to enhance learning outcomes.

Development 
The development of the I.S.L.A. (Instructional Sipnayan Learning Adventure) 
mobile game incorporated a focused set of tools chosen for rapid 2D iteration and 
lightweight cloud integration. Godot 4 served as the primary game engine, with GDScript 
used for all gameplay logic, from NPC behavior to combat resolution. Custom state 
machines governed overall game flow, cleanly separating exploration, dialogue, and battle 
states so that transitions between the island map, story sequences, and math encoun ters 
remained predictable and easy to debug. A GlobalData autoload script functioned as the 
project's central singleton, holding session state, active player progress, and battle metrics 
(accuracy, hints used, timeouts, elapsed time, and win/loss outcomes) that needed to persist 
across scenes without being tied to any single node. Complementing this, a dedicated 
SaveManager handled persistence, writing progress locally as a fallback and synchronizing 
with Firebase Firestore whenever connectivity allowed, so a student's in-game progress, is 
never lost between sessions. The time-limited math combat itself was built around the 
battle.tscn scene, which drives encounters against "Algemon" creatures by presenting 
curriculum-aligned questions which could be modified if needed, evaluating student input 
in real time, and feeding the results back into GlobalData for tracking. Art and UI assets 
were produced or modified with LibreSprite for pixel -based sprite work, Canva for logo 
and branding assets, and Figma for wireframing the game's UI and the companion web 
dashboard before implementation. The system architecture paired the Godot client with a 
web-based teacher dashboard built in Visual Studio Code using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, 
and Node.js, deployed to Vercel for public access. Firebase Authentication secured teacher 
accounts, while Firestore stored the question bank, student scores, and progress analytics, 
giving the dashboard a real -time view into pre- and post-test results synced directly from 
the mobile cl ient. Coding practices favored modularity, with scripts organized by 
responsibility (state machines, GlobalData, SaveManager, UI controllers) so that 
gameplay, persistence, and networking concerns stayed decoupled and testable in isolation. 
The result is an educational game that pairs an engaging time-limited battle system with a 
teacher-facing analytics layer.

Data gathering Methods and Procedures 
The researchers conducted a three -day testing period involving selected Grade 3 
students of Master Hand School Inc., with the assistance of their teachers. Prior to the 
testing, the researchers consulted Ms. Gungon, a Grade 3 teacher, who identified addition, 
subtraction, and multiplication as the mathematics topics already taught to the students. 
These topics served as the basis for the mathematical questions included in the pre -
assessment and post-assessment. The participants were divided into two groups: the control 
group and the experimental group. Both groups completed a pre -assessment to determine 
their initial mathematics performance. 
Following the pre-assessment, the experimental group was provided with a demo version 
of I.S.L.A., which they played using devices provided by the researchers, with assistance 
from the teachers. Meanwhile, the control group did not use the game during the 
intervention period. After the intervention, both groups completed a post -assessment to 
determine changes in their mathematics performance. The control group was then allowed 
to experience the game after completing the post-assessment. Subsequently, the researchers 
distributed a survey questionnaire to both groups to gather feedback regarding the game's 
usability, performance, engagement, and educational effectiveness. The collected 
assessment results and survey responses served as the basis for evaluating the effectiveness 
and acceptability of the developed game.

CONCLUSION 
Summary  
This study developed and evaluated I.S.L.A., a 2D adventure mobile game with a 
web-based analytics dashboard for Grade 3 mathematics at Master Hand School Inc. 
A three -day pilot test was conducted on October 13 –15, 2026, involving 23 students 
divided into an experimental group of 13 and a control group of 10. Both groups completed 
parallel 15 -item pre - and post -assessments, but only the experimental group played 
I.S.L.A. between assessments. The control group played after the post -assessment, and 
both groups answered a 10-item acceptability survey. 
The experimental group achieved a 20.14% relative improvement, meeting the study's 20% 
target, while the control group showed a 5.05% decrease. The pre -assessment difference 
was not statistically significant ( p = .515), while the post -assessment difference was 
significant (p = .001). The survey obtained an overall weighted mean of 4.51, interpreted 
as "Strongly Agree," with enjoyment and ease of understanding receiving the highest 
ratings. 
Conclusions  
1. The researchers conclude that I.S.L.A. was successfully developed and 
implemented as a supplementary learning tool, integrating a mathematics 2D 
adventure game with a web-based analytics dashboard. 
2. The researchers conclude that students who used I.S.L.A.  over three-day period  
demonstrated greater short-term improvement in mathematics performance than the 
control group, meeting the 20% improvement target. These findings suggest the 
game's potential to support mathematics learning, although its long -term 
effectiveness requires further evaluation. 
3. The researchers conclude that I.S.L.A. received high student acceptability ratings, 
particularly for enjoyment and ease of understanding, while map exploration and 
time-limited quiz combat remain areas for improvement. 
 

--- PDF PAGE 100 ---
STI College Caloocan 90 
 
Recommendations 
The project, designed as a supplementary learning tool to support Grade 3 students' 
mathematics performance, has room for further improvement. Based on the students' 
feedback, enhancing map exploration through clearer directions and visual indicators is 
recommended to make navigation easier. Providing adjustable time limits during quiz 
combat could also accommodate students with different problem -solving speeds while 
maintaining the game's ease of use and enjoyment. Additionally, future researchers are 
encouraged to involve more participants in both control and experimental groups and 
extend the testing period according to the school's availability. Conducting follow -up 
assessments could also help determine whether students retain their mathematics learning 
over time and provide stronger evidence of the game's effectiveness alongside regular 
classroom instruction.
